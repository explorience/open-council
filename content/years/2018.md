London City Council maintained an active governance schedule in 2018 with 102 meetings across seven committees, demonstrating the extensive work required to manage a major municipality. The Corporate Services Committee was the busiest with 20 meetings, handling critical administrative functions like budget oversight, human resources, and financial management. City Council itself met 18 times for formal decision-making sessions, while the Planning and Environment Committee also held 18 meetings to address zoning applications, development approvals, and environmental policies that shape the city's growth.

The remaining committees each played vital roles in municipal governance: Civic Works (15 meetings) focused on infrastructure projects, road maintenance, and public works; Community and Protective Services (15 meetings) addressed social services, public safety, and community programs; Strategic Priorities and Policy (13 meetings) worked on long-term planning and policy development; and the Audit Committee (3 meetings) provided financial oversight and accountability. Together, these committees handled the full spectrum of municipal responsibilities, from day-to-day operational decisions to major capital investments, regulatory changes, and strategic planning initiatives that impact residents' quality of life and the city's future development.
