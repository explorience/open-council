London City Council maintained an active governance schedule in 2018 with 102 meetings across seven committees, demonstrating the extensive deliberative work required to manage a major municipality. The Corporate Services Committee was the most active with 20 meetings, handling critical administrative functions including budget oversight, human resources policies, and financial management. The main City Council held 18 meetings for final decision-making on major issues, while the Planning and Environment Committee also met 18 times to address zoning applications, development proposals, environmental policies, and long-term urban planning initiatives.

The remaining committees each played vital specialized roles: Civic Works (15 meetings) focused on infrastructure projects, road maintenance, public works, and transportation planning; Community and Protective Services (15 meetings) addressed social services, public safety, recreation programs, and community development; Strategic Priorities and Policy (13 meetings) guided long-term municipal strategy and policy development; and the Audit Committee (3 meetings) provided financial oversight and accountability. Together, these committees handled the full spectrum of municipal governance, from day-to-day operational decisions to major capital investments, regulatory changes, and strategic planning that shapes London's future development and quality of life for residents.
