London City Council maintained an active governance schedule in 2018 with 102 meetings across seven committees, demonstrating the substantial workload required to manage a major municipal government. The Corporate Services Committee led with 20 meetings, handling critical administrative functions like budget oversight, human resources, and financial management. City Council itself met 18 times for formal decision-making sessions, while the Planning and Environment Committee also convened 18 times to address zoning applications, development approvals, and environmental policy matters that shape the city's growth and sustainability.

The distribution of meetings reflects typical municipal priorities, with committees focusing on community services (15 meetings), infrastructure and public works (15 meetings), and strategic policy development (13 meetings). These committees would have addressed essential civic functions including budget allocation, tax policy, road maintenance, public transit, parks and recreation, emergency services, building permits, and long-term city planning. The Audit Committee's three meetings, while fewer in number, represent crucial oversight ensuring financial accountability and proper governance procedures across all city operations.
