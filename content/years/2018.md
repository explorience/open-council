London City Council maintained an active governance schedule in 2018 with 102 meetings across seven key committees, demonstrating the extensive work required to manage a major municipal government. The Corporate Services Committee led with 20 meetings, handling critical administrative functions like budget oversight, human resources, and financial planning. City Council itself convened 18 times for formal decision-making sessions, while the Planning and Environment Committee matched this frequency as they addressed zoning applications, development approvals, and environmental initiatives that shape the city's growth.

The distribution of committee meetings reflects the breadth of municipal responsibilities, from infrastructure projects and public works managed by the Civic Works Committee (15 meetings) to social services, public safety, and community programs overseen by the Community and Protective Services Committee (15 meetings). The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee's 13 meetings focused on long-term planning and policy development, while the Audit Committee's three meetings provided essential financial oversight. Throughout the year, these committees collectively made decisions on municipal budgets, capital projects, bylaw amendments, development permits, service delivery standards, and strategic initiatives that directly impact residents' daily lives.
