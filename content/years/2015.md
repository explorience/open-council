London City Council's 2015 work demonstrates the extensive deliberative process required to govern a major municipality, with 118 committee and council meetings held throughout the year. The distribution of meetings reflects the council's key priorities and workload, with Corporate Services leading at 29 meetings to handle budgetary matters, financial planning, and administrative oversight, followed closely by Strategic Priorities and Policy at 27 meetings for long-term planning and policy development. The Planning and Environment Committee's 26 meetings indicate significant activity in zoning decisions, development approvals, and environmental initiatives, while Civic Works' 16 meetings likely addressed infrastructure projects, road maintenance, and public works.

The council structure reveals how municipal governments tackle diverse responsibilities through specialized committees before bringing major decisions to the full City Council's four formal meetings. These committees would have addressed typical municipal decisions including annual budget approval, zoning bylaw amendments, infrastructure investments, development permits, public safety policies, and community services delivery. The relatively fewer Audit Committee meetings (4) and Community and Protective Services meetings (12) suggest either more streamlined processes in these areas or that much of the groundwork was handled at the staff level before reaching committee stage.
