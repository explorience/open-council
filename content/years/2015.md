London City Council maintained an active governance schedule in 2015 with 118 total meetings across seven committees, demonstrating the extensive work required to manage a major municipal government. The Corporate Services Committee led with 29 meetings, handling critical administrative matters including budget development, financial oversight, human resources policies, and technology infrastructure decisions. Close behind were the Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee (27 meetings) and Planning and Environment Committee (26 meetings), reflecting the city's focus on long-term planning, development applications, zoning amendments, and environmental stewardship.

The distribution of meetings reveals the multifaceted nature of municipal governance. Civic Works Committee's 16 meetings addressed infrastructure projects, road maintenance, public works, and transportation planning, while the Community and Protective Services Committee's 12 meetings focused on social services, public safety, recreation programs, and health initiatives. With only four formal City Council meetings, most detailed deliberation occurred at the committee level, where specialized knowledge and focused discussion could address complex municipal issues before bringing recommendations to full council for final approval. This structure allows for thorough examination of everything from individual development proposals to multi-million dollar capital projects that shape the city's future.
