London City Council's 2015 operations demonstrate the extensive committee-based work that drives municipal governance, with 118 meetings held across seven key committees. The busiest committees were Corporate Services (29 meetings), Strategic Priorities and Policy (27 meetings), and Planning and Environment (26 meetings), reflecting the council's primary focus areas of financial management, long-term city planning, and development oversight. These committees handle the core municipal responsibilities that directly impact residents' daily lives, from reviewing and approving annual budgets and tax rates to making zoning decisions that shape neighborhood development and evaluating infrastructure projects like road repairs, water system upgrades, and public transit improvements.

The distribution of meetings across committees reveals a typical municipal council's workflow, where specialized committees do the detailed examination of issues before they reach the full City Council for final approval. Community and Protective Services Committee meetings (12) would have addressed public safety, social services, and recreation programs, while Civic Works Committee sessions (16) focused on engineering projects and public works. The relatively fewer full City Council meetings (4) reflects the standard practice where councils meet formally to ratify committee recommendations and handle ceremonial duties, while the Audit Committee's 4 meetings demonstrate ongoing oversight of financial accountability and municipal operations.
