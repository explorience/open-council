London City Council's 2015 work demonstrates the extensive scope and frequency of municipal governance, with 118 total committee and council meetings held throughout the year. The distribution of meetings reveals key priorities, with Corporate Services leading at 29 meetings to handle budget planning, financial oversight, and administrative matters, followed closely by Strategic Priorities and Policy at 27 meetings for long-term planning and policy development. Planning and Environment Committee's 26 meetings reflect the ongoing demands of urban development, addressing zoning applications, development approvals, and environmental assessments that shape the city's growth.

The committee structure shows how municipal councils divide their work across specialized areas: Civic Works Committee's 16 meetings focused on infrastructure projects like road maintenance, water systems, and public works; Community and Protective Services' 12 meetings addressed social services, public health, emergency services, and community programs; while the Audit Committee's 4 meetings provided financial oversight and accountability. With only 4 full City Council meetings, most detailed work occurred at the committee level, where councilors could thoroughly examine issues before bringing recommendations to the full council for final decisions on budgets, bylaws, development permits, and strategic initiatives.
