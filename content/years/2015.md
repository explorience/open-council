London City Council's 2015 operations demonstrate the extensive and diverse nature of municipal governance, with 118 total committee and council meetings addressing the full spectrum of urban administration. The Corporate Services Committee led with 29 meetings, handling critical financial matters including budget deliberations, staff reports, and administrative policies. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee followed closely with 27 meetings, focusing on long-term planning initiatives and policy development, while the Planning and Environment Committee's 26 meetings addressed zoning applications, development proposals, environmental assessments, and urban planning decisions that shape the city's growth.

The remaining committees tackled equally vital municipal functions: Civic Works oversaw infrastructure projects, road maintenance, public works, and transportation planning across 16 meetings, while Community and Protective Services managed social programs, public safety initiatives, housing policies, and emergency services through 12 meetings. The formal City Council sessions, though limited to 4 meetings, served as the decision-making culmination where committee recommendations were ratified into official policy. This committee structure reflects how modern municipalities balance detailed technical review with democratic oversight, ensuring thorough examination of everything from multi-million dollar infrastructure investments to neighborhood zoning changes.
