London City Council's 2015 meeting schedule reflects the comprehensive scope of municipal governance, with 118 meetings held across seven different committees and council sessions. The distribution of meetings shows where the city's primary focus areas lie, with Corporate Services leading at 29 meetings, followed closely by Strategic Priorities and Policy (27 meetings) and Planning and Environment (26 meetings). These committees handle the core functions of municipal government: Corporate Services manages budgets, financial planning, and administrative policies; Strategic Priorities and Policy focuses on long-term city planning and major policy initiatives; while Planning and Environment oversees zoning decisions, development applications, and environmental regulations.

The remaining committees—Civic Works (16 meetings), Community and Protective Services (12 meetings), Audit (4 meetings), and full City Council sessions (4 meetings)—round out the governance structure by addressing infrastructure projects like road maintenance and public works, community programs and public safety services, financial oversight, and formal decision-making on major issues. This meeting distribution demonstrates how municipal councils must balance immediate operational needs with long-term strategic planning, while managing everything from neighborhood zoning disputes to multi-million dollar infrastructure investments that shape the city's future.
