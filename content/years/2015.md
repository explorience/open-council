London City Council's 2015 work demonstrates the extensive deliberative process required to govern a major municipality. With 118 total committee and council meetings throughout the year, the council addressed the full spectrum of municipal responsibilities through specialized committees. The Corporate Services Committee led with 29 meetings, focusing on financial management, human resources, and administrative policies including budget deliberations and fiscal oversight. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee held 27 meetings to guide long-term planning and policy development, while the Planning and Environment Committee's 26 meetings addressed zoning applications, development approvals, environmental assessments, and urban planning initiatives that shape the city's growth.

The remaining committees tackled equally critical municipal functions: Civic Works Committee's 16 meetings covered infrastructure projects, road maintenance, water and sewer systems, and public works contracts, while the Community and Protective Services Committee's 12 meetings dealt with public safety, social services, recreation programs, and community development. The four City Council meetings represented formal sessions where final decisions were ratified after thorough committee review. This committee-intensive structure allows for detailed examination of complex issues ranging from multi-million dollar infrastructure investments to neighborhood zoning changes, ensuring that municipal decisions receive appropriate scrutiny before implementation.
