London City Council's 2015 activities demonstrate the extensive scope of municipal governance, with 118 meetings held across seven committees throughout the year. The workload was primarily concentrated in three key areas: Corporate Services (29 meetings), which typically handles budgetary matters, financial planning, human resources, and administrative policies; Strategic Priorities and Policy (27 meetings), focused on long-term planning, policy development, and strategic initiatives; and Planning and Environment (26 meetings), dealing with zoning applications, development approvals, environmental assessments, and urban planning decisions. These committees form the backbone of municipal decision-making, addressing everything from individual property rezoning requests to city-wide strategic plans.

The remaining committees handled specialized but equally important functions, with Civic Works (16 meetings) addressing infrastructure projects like road construction, utilities, and public works; Community and Protective Services (12 meetings) overseeing social services, public health, emergency services, and community programs; and the Audit Committee (4 meetings) ensuring financial accountability and oversight. Notably, City Council itself met only 4 times, reflecting the committee-based structure where detailed work occurs at the committee level before final ratification by the full council. This distribution suggests 2015 was particularly active in corporate governance, strategic planning, and development-related decisions.
