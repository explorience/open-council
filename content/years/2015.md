London City Council maintained an active schedule in 2015 with 118 meetings across seven committees, demonstrating the extensive work required to govern a major Canadian municipality. The Corporate Services Committee was the busiest with 29 meetings, likely handling critical financial decisions including budget approvals, tax policy, and administrative matters. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee followed closely with 27 meetings, focusing on long-term planning initiatives, economic development strategies, and policy framework development that guides the city's future direction.

The Planning and Environment Committee's 26 meetings reflect the ongoing demands of urban growth management, including zoning amendments, development applications, environmental assessments, and Official Plan updates. Meanwhile, the Civic Works Committee's 16 meetings would have addressed infrastructure needs such as road construction, water and sewer projects, and public works maintenance. The Community and Protective Services Committee's 12 meetings covered essential services like public health, housing, emergency services, and recreation programs. With only 4 formal City Council meetings, much of the detailed work occurred at the committee level, where recommendations were developed before final council approval.
