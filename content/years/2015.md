London City Council's 2015 work demonstrates the extensive committee structure required to govern a major municipality, with 118 meetings held across seven committees throughout the year. The Corporate Services Committee was the most active with 29 meetings, handling crucial administrative functions like budget oversight, financial planning, and human resources policies. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee followed closely with 27 meetings, focusing on long-term planning initiatives and policy development, while the Planning and Environment Committee's 26 meetings addressed zoning applications, development approvals, and environmental regulations that shape the city's growth.

The distribution of meetings reflects the breadth of municipal responsibilities, from the Civic Works Committee's 16 meetings dealing with infrastructure projects like road construction and public works, to the Community and Protective Services Committee's 12 meetings overseeing public safety, social services, and community programs. With only 4 formal City Council meetings, much of the detailed work occurred at the committee level, where specialized knowledge could be applied to complex issues before recommendations reached full council. This structure allows for thorough examination of budgetary decisions, development proposals, infrastructure investments, and policy changes that directly impact residents' daily lives.
