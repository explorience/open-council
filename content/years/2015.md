London City Council's 2015 work demonstrates the extensive deliberative process required to govern a major municipality, with 118 committee and council meetings held throughout the year. The council's work was distributed across specialized committees, with Corporate Services leading at 29 meetings to handle budgets, personnel, and administrative matters, followed closely by Strategic Priorities and Policy (27 meetings) for long-term planning and policy development, and Planning and Environment (26 meetings) for zoning decisions, development approvals, and environmental initiatives. These committees typically address core municipal responsibilities including annual budget approval, infrastructure investment decisions, land use planning, and service delivery policies.

The remaining committees—Civic Works (16 meetings), Community and Protective Services (12 meetings), and Audit (4 meetings)—along with four full City Council meetings, rounded out the governance structure. This meeting distribution reflects typical municipal priorities: managing public works and infrastructure, overseeing community programs and public safety services, ensuring financial accountability, and making final decisions on major policy matters. The relatively fewer full council meetings suggests most detailed work occurs at the committee level, with council sessions reserved for final approvals and major policy decisions.
