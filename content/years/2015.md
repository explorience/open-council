London City Council's 2015 work demonstrates the extensive deliberative process required to govern a major municipality, with 118 committee and council meetings held throughout the year. The committee structure reflects the core areas of municipal responsibility: Corporate Services handled administrative and financial matters including budget deliberations and staff policies; Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee focused on long-term planning and major policy initiatives; while Planning and Environment Committee addressed zoning applications, development approvals, and environmental regulations. These three committees accounted for the majority of meetings, indicating their central role in city governance.

The remaining committees tackled equally vital municipal functions, with Civic Works overseeing infrastructure projects like road construction, water systems, and public works maintenance, while Community and Protective Services managed social programs, public safety, and community development initiatives. The relatively modest number of formal City Council meetings (4) reflects the committee system's effectiveness in conducting detailed work before bringing recommendations to the full council for final approval. This structure allows councillors to develop expertise in specific areas while ensuring thorough review of decisions affecting budgets, land use, infrastructure investments, and service delivery to London's residents.
