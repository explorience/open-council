London City Council's 2016 work demonstrates the extensive committee structure required to govern a major municipality, with 123 meetings held across eight different committees and council itself. The heaviest workloads fell on the Corporate Services Committee and Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee, each holding 28 meetings, suggesting significant focus on administrative matters, financial oversight, and long-term planning initiatives. The Planning and Environment Committee's 21 meetings likely addressed zoning applications, development approvals, and environmental regulations, while the Community and Protective Services Committee and Civic Works Committee each met 16 times to handle matters ranging from public safety and social services to infrastructure maintenance and capital projects.

This meeting distribution reflects the typical scope of municipal government responsibilities, where councils make decisions on annual budgets, property tax rates, zoning bylaws, development permits, infrastructure investments like roads and water systems, public transit, waste management, parks and recreation services, and emergency services. The relatively fewer meetings of the Audit Committee (10) and Budget Committee (1) suggest these bodies focused on specific seasonal responsibilities, with budget deliberations likely concentrated in early 2016 and audit reviews occurring quarterly. Only three formal City Council meetings indicates that much of the detailed work occurred at the committee level, with full council convening primarily for major policy decisions and final approvals.
