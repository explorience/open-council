London City Council's 2016 work demonstrates the extensive and varied nature of municipal governance, with 123 meetings held across eight different committees throughout the year. The bulk of the work occurred in specialized committees, with Corporate Services and Strategic Priorities and Policy Committees each holding 28 meetings, indicating significant focus on administrative oversight and long-term planning. Planning and Environment Committee's 21 meetings reflect the ongoing demands of managing urban development, processing zoning applications, and addressing environmental concerns that are central to a growing city's operations.

The committee structure reveals how municipal councils organize their decision-making around core civic responsibilities: budget planning and financial oversight through the Budget and Audit Committees, infrastructure management via the Civic Works Committee, public safety and social services through Community and Protective Services, and urban development through Planning and Environment. With only three full City Council meetings, this structure shows how detailed policy work happens at the committee level before reaching final council approval, allowing for specialized expertise and thorough examination of complex municipal issues ranging from road repairs and water systems to housing policies and economic development strategies.
