London City Council's 2016 work demonstrates the comprehensive scope of municipal governance, with 123 meetings held across eight committees and council sessions. The majority of the workload was distributed between the Corporate Services Committee and Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee, each holding 28 meetings, indicating significant focus on administrative operations, policy development, and long-term planning initiatives. The Planning and Environment Committee's 21 meetings reflect the ongoing demands of urban development, including zoning decisions, development applications, environmental assessments, and land use planning that shapes the city's growth.

The remaining committees addressed crucial municipal functions through their regular meetings: Community and Protective Services handled public safety, social services, and community programs; Civic Works managed infrastructure projects, road maintenance, and public works; while the Audit Committee provided financial oversight through 10 meetings. Notably, the formal City Council met only 3 times, with a single Budget Committee meeting, suggesting that much of the detailed work occurred at the committee level before final council approval. This structure allows for specialized examination of complex issues ranging from budget allocation and tax policies to development permits, infrastructure investments, and service delivery standards that directly impact residents' daily lives.
