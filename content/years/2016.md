London City Council's 2016 work demonstrates the extensive behind-the-scenes effort required to govern a municipality, with 123 meetings held across eight different committees and council bodies. The bulk of the work occurred in specialized committees, with Corporate Services and Strategic Priorities and Policy Committees each holding 28 meetings to handle core administrative functions, long-term planning, and policy development. The Planning and Environment Committee's 21 meetings reflect the ongoing nature of urban development, processing zoning applications, development proposals, and environmental assessments that shape the city's growth.

Municipal councils like London's make decisions that directly impact residents' daily lives, from setting annual budgets that determine tax rates and service levels, to approving infrastructure projects for roads, water systems, and public facilities. They handle zoning changes that affect neighborhood character, oversee protective services like police and fire departments, and establish policies on everything from business licensing to environmental protection. The committee structure allows for specialized focus—with Civic Works addressing infrastructure needs, Community and Protective Services handling social programs and public safety, and the Audit Committee ensuring financial accountability—before major decisions advance to full City Council for final approval.
