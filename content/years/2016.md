London City Council's 2016 operations demonstrate the extensive committee work that underpins municipal governance, with 123 meetings held across eight different bodies. The bulk of council business was conducted through specialized committees, with Corporate Services and Strategic Priorities and Policy Committees each holding 28 meetings - nearly one-quarter of all sessions. These committees typically handle core municipal functions including budget oversight, strategic planning, human resources, and policy development. The Planning and Environment Committee's 21 meetings reflect the ongoing work of managing urban development through zoning decisions, development applications, and environmental assessments, while the Community and Protective Services Committee's 16 meetings likely addressed public safety, social services, and community programs.

The distribution of meetings reveals how municipal decision-making flows from specialized committees to full Council, with only 3 formal City Council meetings suggesting that most detailed work occurs at the committee level before final ratification. Infrastructure planning and maintenance would have been central to the Civic Works Committee's 16 meetings, covering everything from road repairs and public transit to water and waste management systems. The single Budget Committee meeting, supplemented by extensive work in other committees, reflects the year-long process of developing and monitoring the municipal budget that funds all city operations and services.
