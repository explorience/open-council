London City Council's 2016 work demonstrates the complex, multi-layered governance structure required to manage a major municipality. With 123 meetings held across eight different committees, the council addressed the full spectrum of municipal responsibilities. The Corporate Services and Strategic Priorities and Policy committees were the most active with 28 meetings each, handling essential functions like financial management, human resources, technology infrastructure, and long-term strategic planning. The Planning and Environment Committee's 21 meetings focused on critical urban development decisions including zoning applications, subdivision approvals, environmental assessments, and implementation of the city's official plan.

The remaining committees tackled equally vital municipal services: Community and Protective Services oversaw public safety, social services, and community programs through 16 meetings, while Civic Works managed infrastructure projects, transportation, utilities, and public works with an equal number of sessions. The Audit Committee's 10 meetings ensured financial oversight and accountability. Notably, full City Council convened only 3 times, typically for major ceremonial functions and final ratification of significant decisions, while the Budget Committee's single meeting likely represented the culmination of year-long budget preparation across all other committees before final council approval.
