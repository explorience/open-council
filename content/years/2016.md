London City Council's 2016 work demonstrates the extensive committee structure required to govern a modern city, with 123 meetings held across eight different committees and council itself. The majority of the detailed work occurred in specialized committees, with Corporate Services and Strategic Priorities and Policy Committees each holding 28 meetings - the most active bodies that year. These committees handle the core functions of municipal government: Corporate Services typically manages financial oversight, human resources, and administrative policies, while Strategic Priorities and Policy focuses on long-term planning and major policy initiatives.

The remaining committees reflect the breadth of municipal responsibilities, from Planning and Environment Committee's 21 meetings dealing with zoning applications, development approvals, and environmental policies, to Community and Protective Services' 16 meetings addressing public safety, social services, and community programs. Civic Works Committee's 16 meetings would have covered infrastructure projects, roads, utilities, and public works. Notably, City Council itself met only three times, suggesting most decision-making occurred at the committee level with formal ratification happening at full council meetings. The single Budget Committee meeting likely represents the culmination of year-long budget preparation work done across various committees.
