London City Council's 2016 work demonstrates the intensive, multifaceted nature of municipal governance, with 123 committee meetings addressing the full spectrum of urban management. The Corporate Services and Strategic Priorities and Policy committees led the workload with 28 meetings each, handling critical functions like budget oversight, human resources, strategic planning, and policy development. Meanwhile, the Planning and Environment Committee's 21 meetings focused on zoning decisions, development applications, and environmental initiatives that shape the city's growth and sustainability.

The remaining committees tackled equally vital municipal responsibilities: Community and Protective Services addressed public safety, social services, and community programs through 16 meetings, while Civic Works managed infrastructure projects, transportation, and public works with another 16 meetings. The Audit Committee's 10 meetings ensured financial accountability and transparency. Notably, formal City Council sessions were limited to just 3 meetings, with one dedicated Budget Committee meeting, suggesting most detailed work occurred at the committee level before reaching full council for final decisions on matters ranging from tax rates and development permits to road construction and emergency services.
