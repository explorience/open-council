London City Council's 2016 work demonstrates the extensive committee structure required to manage a modern municipality, with 123 total meetings held across eight different committees and council itself. The heaviest workloads fell on the Corporate Services Committee and Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee, each holding 28 meetings to address the city's administrative operations, long-term planning initiatives, and policy development. The Planning and Environment Committee's 21 meetings reflect the ongoing demands of urban development, including zoning decisions, environmental assessments, and development approvals that shape the city's growth.

The remaining committees handled specialized but equally critical municipal functions: Community and Protective Services and Civic Works each met 16 times to address social services, public safety, infrastructure maintenance, and capital projects like road construction and utility upgrades. The Audit Committee's 10 meetings ensured financial oversight and accountability, while the single Budget Committee meeting likely focused on the annual budget approval process. Notably, City Council itself met only three times, suggesting that much of the detailed decision-making occurred at the committee level, with full council meetings reserved for final approvals and major policy decisions.
