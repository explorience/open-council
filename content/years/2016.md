London City Council's 2016 work demonstrates the extensive committee structure required to govern a major city, with 123 meetings held across eight specialized committees. The heaviest workload fell on the Corporate Services Committee and Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee, each holding 28 meetings, indicating significant focus on administrative operations, human resources, financial management, and long-term municipal planning. The Planning and Environment Committee's 21 meetings reflect the ongoing need to review development applications, zoning changes, and environmental assessments that shape the city's growth and sustainability.

The distribution of meetings across committees illustrates how municipal governance addresses diverse community needs through specialized expertise. While City Council itself met only three times for formal decision-making, the bulk of detailed work occurred in committees handling infrastructure projects (Civic Works), social services and public safety (Community and Protective Services), and financial oversight (Audit Committee). This structure allows councillors to thoroughly examine complex issues like annual budgets, development proposals, road construction projects, bylaw amendments, and service delivery improvements before bringing recommendations to the full council for final approval.
