London City Council's 2016 work demonstrates the extensive committee structure required to govern a major municipality, with 123 meetings held across eight different committees and council sessions. The heaviest workload fell on the Corporate Services Committee and Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee, each holding 28 meetings, indicating significant focus on administrative oversight, long-term planning, and policy development. The Planning and Environment Committee's 21 meetings reflect the ongoing demands of urban development, including zoning decisions, development applications, and environmental assessments that shape the city's growth.

The distribution of meetings across committees reveals the breadth of municipal responsibilities, from the Community and Protective Services Committee and Civic Works Committee (16 meetings each) handling essential services like public safety, recreation programs, and infrastructure maintenance, to the Audit Committee's 10 meetings ensuring financial accountability. Interestingly, City Council itself met only 3 times, suggesting most detailed work occurred at the committee level before formal ratification. The single Budget Committee meeting likely represents the culmination of extensive budget preparation work done throughout the year in other committees, addressing capital projects, operating expenses, and tax policy decisions that directly impact residents' daily lives.
