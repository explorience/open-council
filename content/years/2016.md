London City Council's 2016 work demonstrates the extensive and diverse nature of municipal governance, with 123 meetings held across eight different committees and council sessions. The heaviest workload fell on the Corporate Services Committee and Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee, each holding 28 meetings, suggesting significant focus on administrative oversight, long-term planning, and policy development. The Planning and Environment Committee's 21 meetings reflect the ongoing demands of urban development, including zoning decisions, development applications, and environmental assessments that shape the city's growth.

The distribution of meetings across committees illustrates how municipal councils address the full spectrum of local government responsibilities. From infrastructure decisions handled by the Civic Works Committee to public safety and social services overseen by Community and Protective Services, council work encompasses budget approvals, bylaw creation, service delivery oversight, and strategic planning. The relatively few formal City Council meetings (3) compared to committee work shows how much of the detailed decision-making occurs at the committee level before reaching full council for final approval, while the single Budget Committee meeting likely represents the culmination of extensive budget preparation work done throughout other committees.
