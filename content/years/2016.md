London City Council's 2016 work demonstrates the extensive committee structure required to govern a major municipality, with 123 meetings held across eight different committees and council itself. The heaviest workload fell on the Corporate Services Committee and Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee, each holding 28 meetings throughout the year, indicating significant focus on administrative operations, long-term planning, and policy development. The Planning and Environment Committee's 21 meetings reflect the ongoing demands of managing urban development, zoning applications, and environmental initiatives that are crucial to a growing city.

The distribution of meetings across committees reveals the breadth of municipal responsibilities, from infrastructure decisions handled by the Civic Works Committee to public safety and social services overseen by the Community and Protective Services Committee. With only three formal City Council meetings and one Budget Committee meeting, much of the detailed work clearly occurred at the committee level, where councilors examined specific issues like development permits, road construction projects, utility expansions, bylaw amendments, and service delivery improvements before bringing recommendations to full council for final approval.
