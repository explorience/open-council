London City Council maintained an active governance schedule in 2016 with 123 meetings across eight different committees, demonstrating the comprehensive scope of municipal administration. The Corporate Services and Strategic Priorities and Policy committees led the workload with 28 meetings each, handling crucial decisions around financial management, human resources, strategic planning, and long-term policy development. The Planning and Environment Committee, with 21 meetings, focused on zoning applications, development approvals, and environmental initiatives that shape the city's growth and sustainability efforts.

The remaining committees addressed specialized areas of municipal responsibility: Community and Protective Services oversaw public safety, social services, and recreational programs; Civic Works managed infrastructure projects including roads, utilities, and public works; while the Audit Committee ensured financial oversight and accountability. Notably, City Council held only three formal meetings, suggesting most detailed work occurred at the committee level before final ratification. This structure allowed councillors to thoroughly examine complex issues like annual budgets, development proposals, infrastructure investments, and policy reforms before bringing recommendations to the full council for final approval.
