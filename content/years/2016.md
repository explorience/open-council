London City Council's 2016 work demonstrates the extensive deliberative process required to govern a major municipality, with 123 committee and council meetings held throughout the year. The distribution of meetings reflects key municipal priorities: Corporate Services and Strategic Priorities and Policy Committees each held the most meetings (28), indicating significant focus on administrative oversight, long-term planning, and policy development. Planning and Environment Committee's 21 meetings highlight the ongoing work of managing urban development, zoning applications, and environmental initiatives that shape the city's growth and sustainability.

The remaining committees address core municipal services that directly impact residents' daily lives. Community and Protective Services and Civic Works Committees each met 16 times to oversee public safety, social programs, infrastructure maintenance, and capital projects like road construction and utility services. The Audit Committee's 10 meetings ensured financial accountability and governance oversight, while the single Budget Committee meeting represents the culmination of year-long budget preparation across all departments. These committees collectively make decisions on everything from rezoning applications and development permits to emergency services funding, park improvements, and major infrastructure investments that determine how taxpayer dollars are allocated to serve London's growing population.
