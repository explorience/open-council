London City Council's 2016 work demonstrates the complex, multi-faceted nature of municipal governance, with 123 committee meetings addressing the diverse needs of the city. The council's work was distributed across specialized committees, each handling distinct areas of municipal responsibility. The Corporate Services and Strategic Priorities and Policy Committees were the most active with 28 meetings each, reflecting the significant time dedicated to administrative oversight, policy development, and long-term planning initiatives. The Planning and Environment Committee's 21 meetings addressed critical urban development decisions including zoning amendments, subdivision approvals, and environmental compliance issues that shape the city's growth.

The remaining committees focused on essential municipal services: Community and Protective Services addressed public safety, social programs, and emergency services; Civic Works managed infrastructure projects, road maintenance, and public utilities; while the Audit Committee provided financial oversight through 10 meetings. Notably, the full City Council convened only 3 times for formal sessions, with much of the detailed work occurring at the committee level where specialized expertise could be applied to complex issues like budget allocations, development permits, infrastructure investments, and policy implementation. This structure allows for thorough examination of matters before they reach final council approval.
