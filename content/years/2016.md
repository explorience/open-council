London City Council's 2016 work demonstrates the comprehensive scope of municipal governance, with 123 meetings held across eight specialized committees and full council sessions. The heaviest workload fell on the Corporate Services Committee and Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee, each holding 28 meetings to address core administrative functions, long-term planning initiatives, and policy development. The Planning and Environment Committee's 21 meetings reflect the ongoing demands of urban development, including zoning decisions, development approvals, and environmental assessments that shape the city's growth and sustainability.

The distribution of meetings across committees illustrates how municipal councils balance immediate service delivery with strategic planning. Community and Protective Services and Civic Works committees each met 16 times to address essential services like public safety, social programs, parks, roads, and infrastructure maintenance. Meanwhile, the Audit Committee's 10 meetings ensured financial oversight and accountability. Notably, with only three full City Council meetings and one Budget Committee session, much of the detailed work occurred at the committee level before formal ratification, allowing for specialized focus on complex issues ranging from multi-million dollar infrastructure projects to neighborhood zoning changes.
