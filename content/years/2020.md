London City Council's 2020 work demonstrates the extensive scope of municipal governance, with 112 meetings held across seven key committees. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee led with 24 meetings, reflecting the significant time dedicated to long-term planning and policy development, while regular City Council sessions (21 meetings) served as the central decision-making body. The high activity levels in Corporate Services (19 meetings) and Planning and Environment (19 meetings) committees highlight two critical areas of municipal work: managing city finances, human resources, and administrative operations, alongside reviewing development applications, zoning changes, and environmental initiatives.

Throughout these meetings, council members tackled the fundamental responsibilities of municipal government, including setting annual budgets that allocate taxpayer dollars across city services, reviewing and approving zoning amendments and development proposals that shape the city's growth, and making decisions about infrastructure projects like road maintenance, water systems, and public facilities. The Community and Protective Services and Civic Works committees, each holding 13 meetings, focused on essential services such as public health, social programs, emergency services, transportation, and utilities. The lighter schedule of the Audit Committee (3 meetings) reflects its specialized role in financial oversight and accountability, ensuring proper use of public funds and adherence to municipal policies.
