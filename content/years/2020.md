London City Council's 2020 work demonstrates the comprehensive scope of municipal governance, with 112 meetings held across seven specialized committees throughout the year. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee led with 24 meetings, reflecting the need for extensive policy development and strategic planning, while regular City Council sessions (21 meetings) served as the primary decision-making body where final votes on major initiatives took place. The Corporate Services and Planning and Environment committees each held 19 meetings, handling critical areas such as budget oversight, financial management, development applications, zoning amendments, and environmental policies.

A typical year of council work involves making decisions that directly impact residents' daily lives, from approving annual operating and capital budgets that determine tax rates and service levels, to reviewing zoning applications and development proposals that shape the city's growth. Committees address infrastructure needs like road maintenance, water systems, and public transit, while also overseeing community services, public safety initiatives, and protective services. The relatively fewer Audit Committee meetings (3) reflect its focused mandate on financial oversight and accountability, while the Community and Protective Services and Civic Works committees each held 13 meetings to address essential services ranging from social programs and emergency services to public works and infrastructure maintenance.
