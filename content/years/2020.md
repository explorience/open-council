London City Council maintained an active governance schedule in 2020 with 112 meetings across seven key committees, demonstrating the extensive work required to manage a major municipality. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee led with 24 meetings, reflecting its central role in setting the city's long-term direction and coordinating major policy initiatives. Regular City Council meetings (21) served as the primary decision-making body where elected officials vote on bylaws, budgets, and major municipal matters, while specialized committees handled specific portfolios including corporate services, planning and environment, community and protective services, and civic works.

Throughout these meetings, council members would have addressed the full spectrum of municipal responsibilities that directly impact residents' daily lives. This includes setting annual budgets and tax rates, making zoning decisions for new developments, approving infrastructure projects like road repairs and public transit improvements, establishing community programs and safety services, and responding to environmental concerns. The committee structure allows for detailed examination of complex issues before they reach the full council for final approval, ensuring thorough deliberation on everything from snow removal contracts to affordable housing strategies to economic development initiatives.
