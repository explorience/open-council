London City Council's 2014 work demonstrates the extensive scope of municipal governance, with 109 meetings held across seven committees throughout the year. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee led the meeting count with 24 sessions, focusing on the city's long-term direction, budget planning, and major policy initiatives. Corporate Services (23 meetings) and Planning and Environment (22 meetings) were equally active, handling essential functions like financial management, human resources, development applications, zoning changes, and environmental regulations that shape the city's growth and sustainability.

The remaining committees addressed critical municipal services: Community and Protective Services (18 meetings) oversaw public safety, social programs, and community development; Civic Works (17 meetings) managed infrastructure projects, road maintenance, and public utilities; while the Audit Committee (4 meetings) provided financial oversight. Notably, formal City Council meetings were limited to just one session, suggesting most decision-making occurred at the committee level before formal ratification. This structure allows for specialized focus on complex municipal issues ranging from budget allocations and infrastructure investments to zoning approvals and service delivery improvements.
