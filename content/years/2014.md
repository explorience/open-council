London City Council's 2014 work demonstrates the extensive committee structure required to govern a major municipality, with 109 meetings held across seven committees and council. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee led with 24 meetings, reflecting the significant time devoted to setting long-term municipal direction, developing strategic plans, and making high-level policy decisions that guide the city's future. Corporate Services (23 meetings) and Planning and Environment (22 meetings) committees also met frequently, handling critical areas like budget deliberations, financial oversight, human resources, zoning applications, development approvals, and environmental initiatives.

The remaining committees addressed the day-to-day operations that keep the city functioning effectively. Community and Protective Services (18 meetings) likely dealt with social services, public health, emergency services, and community programs, while Civic Works (17 meetings) focused on infrastructure projects, road maintenance, water and sewer systems, and public works. The Audit Committee's four meetings provided essential financial oversight and accountability. Interestingly, only one formal City Council meeting was recorded, suggesting that much of the detailed work occurred in committees, with council serving primarily as the final decision-making body for recommendations forwarded from the various committees.
