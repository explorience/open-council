London City Council's 2014 work demonstrates the comprehensive scope of municipal governance, with 109 committee meetings addressing the city's diverse needs. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee led with 24 meetings, focusing on long-term planning, budget development, and policy frameworks that guide the city's direction. Corporate Services (23 meetings) and Planning and Environment (22 meetings) were equally active, handling essential functions like financial management, human resources, zoning applications, development approvals, and environmental regulations that shape how the city grows and operates.

The remaining committees tackled equally vital municipal responsibilities: Community and Protective Services (18 meetings) addressed public safety, social services, recreation programs, and community health initiatives, while Civic Works (17 meetings) managed infrastructure projects, road maintenance, utilities, and public works that keep the city functioning. The Audit Committee's 4 meetings provided financial oversight and accountability. Notably, only 1 formal City Council meeting was recorded, suggesting most decision-making occurred at the committee level before final ratification, reflecting an efficient governance structure that allows for specialized expertise and thorough deliberation on complex municipal issues.
