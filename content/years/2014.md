London City Council's 2014 work demonstrates the extensive committee structure that underpins municipal governance, with 109 meetings held across seven committees and council. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee led activity with 24 meetings, reflecting its role in setting the city's long-term direction, budget priorities, and major policy initiatives. Corporate Services (23 meetings) and Planning and Environment (22 meetings) were nearly as active, handling critical functions like financial management, human resources, development applications, zoning changes, and environmental regulations.

The remaining committees addressed core municipal services: Community and Protective Services (18 meetings) dealt with public safety, social services, and recreation programs, while Civic Works (17 meetings) managed infrastructure projects, roads, utilities, and public works. The Audit Committee's 4 meetings focused on financial oversight and accountability. Notably, only 1 formal City Council meeting was recorded, suggesting most decision-making occurred at the committee level before ratification. This structure allows councillors to specialize in specific areas while ensuring comprehensive oversight of budgets, development approvals, infrastructure investments, service delivery, and regulatory matters that directly impact residents' daily lives.
