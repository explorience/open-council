London City Council's 2014 work demonstrates the extensive committee structure required to govern a major municipality, with 109 meetings held across seven committees throughout the year. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee led with 24 meetings, focusing on long-term planning and major policy initiatives, while the Corporate Services Committee held 23 meetings to address financial management, human resources, and administrative matters including budget deliberations. The Planning and Environment Committee's 22 meetings handled crucial decisions around zoning applications, development approvals, environmental assessments, and urban planning initiatives that shape the city's growth.

The remaining committees addressed equally vital municipal functions: Community and Protective Services (18 meetings) dealt with public safety, social services, recreation programs, and community development; Civic Works (17 meetings) managed infrastructure projects, road maintenance, utilities, and public works; while the Audit Committee's 4 meetings provided financial oversight and accountability. Interestingly, only one formal City Council meeting was recorded, suggesting that most decision-making occurred at the committee level before reaching full council for final approval. This structure allows for specialized expertise and detailed examination of complex municipal issues ranging from multi-million dollar infrastructure investments to neighborhood zoning changes.
