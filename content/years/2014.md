London City Council's 2014 work demonstrates the extensive committee structure required to govern a major Canadian city, with 109 meetings held across seven different committees and council itself. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee led with 24 meetings, focusing on long-term planning and policy development, while the Corporate Services Committee held 23 meetings to address budgetary matters, financial oversight, and administrative operations. The Planning and Environment Committee's 22 meetings would have addressed crucial zoning decisions, development applications, environmental assessments, and urban planning initiatives that shape the city's growth.

The remaining committees reflect the breadth of municipal responsibilities: Community and Protective Services (18 meetings) handling public safety, social services, and community programs; Civic Works (17 meetings) managing infrastructure projects, roads, utilities, and public works; and the Audit Committee (4 meetings) providing financial oversight and accountability. Interestingly, City Council itself met only once, suggesting that most detailed work occurred at the committee level, with committees making recommendations that were likely ratified in a consolidated council session. This structure allows for specialized expertise and thorough examination of complex issues ranging from annual budget approval and capital infrastructure investments to development permits and service delivery improvements.
