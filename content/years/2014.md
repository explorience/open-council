London City Council's 2014 work demonstrates the comprehensive scope of municipal governance, with 109 committee meetings addressing the diverse needs of the city. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee led with 24 meetings, focusing on long-term planning, budget development, and major policy initiatives that guide the city's direction. Corporate Services (23 meetings) and Planning and Environment (22 meetings) were equally active, handling essential functions like financial management, human resources, development applications, zoning bylaws, and environmental regulations that directly impact residents' daily lives.

The remaining committees tackled equally important municipal responsibilities: Community and Protective Services (18 meetings) addressed public safety, social services, recreation programs, and emergency services, while Civic Works (17 meetings) managed infrastructure projects, road maintenance, utilities, and public works. The Audit Committee's 4 meetings provided crucial financial oversight, and the single City Council meeting likely represented a formal ratification session. This meeting distribution reflects how modern municipal government operates through specialized committees that develop expertise in specific areas before bringing recommendations to full council for final approval.
