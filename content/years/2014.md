London City Council maintained an active governance schedule in 2014 with 109 meetings across seven committees, demonstrating the extensive work required to manage a major municipal government. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee led with 24 meetings, focusing on long-term planning, budget oversight, and policy development that guides the city's direction. Corporate Services (23 meetings) and Planning and Environment (22 meetings) were equally busy, handling essential functions like financial management, human resources, development approvals, zoning changes, and environmental regulations that directly impact residents' daily lives.

The remaining committees addressed core municipal services through regular meetings: Community and Protective Services (18 meetings) managed social programs, public safety, and community well-being initiatives, while Civic Works (17 meetings) oversaw critical infrastructure projects including road maintenance, water systems, and public works. Throughout these committees, council members made decisions on annual budgets, development permits, infrastructure investments, service delivery standards, and policy frameworks. The Audit Committee's 4 meetings ensured financial accountability and transparency. Notably, only 1 full City Council meeting was recorded, suggesting most detailed work occurred at the committee level before formal ratification.
