London City Council's 2014 activities demonstrate the comprehensive scope of municipal governance, with 109 meetings held across seven committees and council itself. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee led with 24 meetings, reflecting the significant time spent on long-term planning, budget deliberations, and policy development that guides the city's direction. Corporate Services (23 meetings) and Planning and Environment (22 meetings) were equally active, handling essential functions like financial management, human resources, development applications, zoning decisions, and environmental regulations that shape how the city grows and operates.

The distribution of meetings across committees reveals the breadth of municipal responsibilities. Community and Protective Services (18 meetings) addressed public safety, social services, and recreational programs, while Civic Works (17 meetings) focused on infrastructure projects, road maintenance, water systems, and public works that keep the city functioning. The Audit Committee's four meetings provided financial oversight and accountability. This committee structure allows council to thoroughly examine complex issues—from annual budget approvals and development permit applications to infrastructure investments and service delivery—before bringing recommendations to the full council for final decisions.
