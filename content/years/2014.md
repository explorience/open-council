London City Council's 2014 work demonstrates the extensive committee structure required to govern a major city, with 109 total meetings held across seven committees and full council. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee led activity with 24 meetings, focusing on long-term planning and major policy directions, while the Corporate Services Committee held 23 meetings to oversee financial management, human resources, and administrative functions including budget deliberations. The Planning and Environment Committee's 22 meetings addressed critical urban development issues such as zoning amendments, subdivision approvals, environmental assessments, and official plan updates that shape the city's growth.

The remaining committees tackled equally important municipal responsibilities: Community and Protective Services (18 meetings) handled public health, social services, emergency services, and community programs; Civic Works (17 meetings) managed infrastructure projects including roads, water systems, waste management, and public transit; and the Audit Committee (4 meetings) provided financial oversight and accountability. Notably, only one full City Council meeting was recorded, suggesting most decision-making occurred at the committee level before ratification. This structure allows councillors to develop specialized expertise while ensuring comprehensive coverage of municipal responsibilities from budget approval to infrastructure investment to regulatory decisions that affect residents' daily lives.
