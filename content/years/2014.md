London City Council's 2014 work demonstrates the extensive committee structure required to govern a major municipality, with 109 meetings held across seven different committees and council bodies. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee led with 24 meetings, reflecting the significant time devoted to long-term planning, budget deliberations, and major policy initiatives that shape the city's direction. Corporate Services (23 meetings) and Planning and Environment (22 meetings) were equally active, handling crucial decisions around municipal finances, human resources, development applications, zoning bylaw amendments, and environmental regulations that directly impact residents' daily lives.

The remaining committees addressed the core services that define municipal governance: Community and Protective Services (18 meetings) dealt with public safety, social programs, and community development, while Civic Works (17 meetings) focused on infrastructure maintenance, road construction, water systems, and public works projects. The Audit Committee's 4 meetings provided financial oversight and accountability. Interestingly, only 1 formal City Council meeting was recorded, suggesting that most decision-making occurred at the committee level before being ratified by council, which is typical of the committee-based governance model used by many Canadian municipalities.
