London City Council's 2014 work demonstrates the extensive committee structure required to govern a major municipality, with 109 meetings held across seven key bodies. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee led activity with 24 meetings, reflecting the significant time devoted to setting municipal direction, budget deliberations, and policy development. The Corporate Services Committee (23 meetings) and Planning and Environment Committee (22 meetings) were equally active, handling essential functions like financial management, human resources, development applications, zoning amendments, and environmental initiatives.

The remaining committees addressed core municipal services through focused meetings: Community and Protective Services (18 meetings) oversaw public safety, social services, and community programs; Civic Works (17 meetings) managed infrastructure projects, roads, utilities, and public works; and the Audit Committee (4 meetings) provided financial oversight. Notably, only one formal City Council meeting was recorded, suggesting most decision-making occurred at the committee level before ratification. Throughout these meetings, councillors would have addressed typical municipal responsibilities including annual budget approval, development and zoning decisions, infrastructure investments, service delivery standards, and policy responses to community needs.
