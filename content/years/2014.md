London City Council's 2014 work demonstrates the extensive behind-the-scenes effort required to govern a major municipality, with 109 committee meetings held throughout the year to address the city's diverse needs. The committee structure reflects the broad scope of municipal responsibilities, from the Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee's 24 meetings focused on long-term planning and policy development, to the Corporate Services Committee's 23 meetings handling financial management, budgets, and administrative operations. The Planning and Environment Committee's 22 meetings dealt with critical decisions around zoning applications, development approvals, environmental assessments, and urban planning initiatives that shape the city's growth and character.

The remaining committees tackled equally important municipal functions, with Community and Protective Services addressing public safety, social services, and community programs through 18 meetings, while Civic Works held 17 meetings to oversee infrastructure projects, road maintenance, water and sewer systems, and public works. The Audit Committee's 4 meetings provided financial oversight and accountability. Notably, formal City Council meetings were minimal, suggesting that most substantive work occurred at the committee level before being brought forward for final approval, reflecting an efficient division of labor that allows elected officials to develop expertise in specific areas while ensuring comprehensive municipal governance.
