London City Council's 2014 work demonstrates the extensive committee structure required to govern a major municipality, with 109 meetings held across seven different bodies. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee led activity with 24 meetings, focusing on long-term planning and policy development, while Corporate Services (23 meetings) handled financial matters including budget deliberations, procurement, and administrative oversight. The Planning and Environment Committee's 22 meetings addressed crucial urban development issues such as zoning applications, subdivision approvals, and environmental assessments that shape the city's growth.

The remaining committees tackled equally important municipal responsibilities: Community and Protective Services (18 meetings) dealt with public safety, social services, and community programs; Civic Works (17 meetings) managed infrastructure projects, road maintenance, and public utilities; and the Audit Committee (4 meetings) provided financial oversight and accountability. Interestingly, City Council itself held only one formal meeting, suggesting that most detailed work occurred at the committee level before coming to full council for final approval. This structure allows for specialized focus on complex municipal issues ranging from multi-million dollar infrastructure investments to neighbourhood zoning changes that affect residents' daily lives.
