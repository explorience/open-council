London City Council's 2012 operations demonstrate the breadth and complexity of municipal governance, with 80 meetings held across seven different committees throughout the year. The Planning and Environment Committee was by far the most active, conducting 29 meetings to address the city's ongoing development needs, zoning applications, environmental assessments, and land use planning decisions. The Civic Works Committee, with 18 meetings, focused on critical infrastructure matters including road construction, public transit, water and sewer systems, and other essential municipal services that keep the city functioning.

The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee's 16 meetings indicate significant work on long-term city planning, budget deliberations, and policy development, while the 10 full City Council meetings served as the formal decision-making body where major bylaws, budget approvals, and policy directions were finalized. The remaining committees - Audit, Community and Protective Services, and Corporate Services - met less frequently but handled specialized areas such as financial oversight, public safety, social services, and internal administrative matters. This meeting structure reflects a typical municipal year where councils balance immediate operational needs with long-term strategic planning, managing everything from individual development applications to multi-million dollar infrastructure projects and annual budget processes.
