London City Council maintained an active governance schedule in 2025, holding 87 meetings across seven specialized committees throughout the year. The council's work was distributed fairly evenly among its core committees, with Planning and Environment, Corporate Services, Community and Protective Services, and full City Council each meeting 16 times to address the city's ongoing operational needs. These committees tackled the fundamental responsibilities of municipal government, including reviewing development applications and zoning changes, managing city finances and human resources, overseeing public safety services like police and fire protection, and making final decisions on city-wide policies and bylaws.

The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee met 13 times to focus on long-term planning initiatives, while the Budget Committee's 6 meetings concentrated on the crucial annual budget process, reviewing departmental spending requests, setting tax rates, and allocating resources for infrastructure projects like road repairs, transit improvements, and facility upgrades. The Audit Committee's 4 meetings provided essential oversight of the city's financial controls and accountability measures. This meeting structure reflects the complex, multi-layered nature of municipal governance, where decisions range from individual development permits and business licenses to major policy initiatives affecting housing, transportation, environmental sustainability, and economic development across London's diverse neighborhoods and business districts.
