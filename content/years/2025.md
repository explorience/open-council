London City Council maintained an active governance schedule in 2025 with 85 meetings across seven specialized committees, demonstrating the comprehensive scope of municipal decision-making. The Corporate Services and Community and Protective Services committees led the workload with 16 meetings each, handling critical areas such as human resources, financial management, public safety, recreation services, and community programs. City Council itself met 16 times for formal votes and policy decisions, while the Planning and Environment Committee's 15 meetings reflected ongoing urban development pressures and environmental initiatives.

The committee structure reveals the diverse responsibilities of municipal government, from the Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee's 13 meetings focused on long-term city planning and major policy initiatives, to the Budget Committee's concentrated 6-meeting schedule for approving annual spending plans and tax rates. Throughout these meetings, councillors would have made decisions on zoning applications and development approvals, infrastructure investments in roads and utilities, budget allocations for city services, bylaw updates, and responses to community concerns. The Audit Committee's 3 meetings ensured financial oversight and accountability, completing the governance framework that keeps Canada's 11th largest city running effectively.
