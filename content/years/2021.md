London City Council maintained an active schedule in 2021 with 99 meetings across seven committees, demonstrating the extensive work required to govern a major municipality. The Corporate Services and Planning and Environment committees led with 17 meetings each, reflecting the heavy focus on financial management, human resources, and development approvals that form the backbone of municipal operations. City Council itself convened 16 times for formal decision-making, while the Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee matched this frequency as councillors worked to set long-term direction for the city.

The distribution of meetings across committees reveals the breadth of municipal responsibilities, from the Civic Works Committee's 15 meetings addressing infrastructure projects, road maintenance, and public works, to the Community and Protective Services Committee's 14 meetings handling social services, public health, and emergency services. Throughout these sessions, councillors would have deliberated on critical decisions including annual budget allocations, zoning amendments and development applications, infrastructure investments, policy frameworks, and service delivery standards. The Audit Committee's four meetings, while fewer in number, provided essential oversight of financial controls and accountability measures that underpin responsible governance.
