London City Council's 2013 work demonstrates the extensive deliberative process required to govern a major municipality, with 122 committee and council meetings held throughout the year. The meeting distribution reflects the core functions of municipal government, with Corporate Services leading at 30 meetings to handle administrative operations, human resources, and financial oversight, followed closely by Planning and Environment at 26 meetings to address zoning applications, development proposals, and environmental regulations. Community and Protective Services held 22 meetings to oversee public safety, social services, and community programs, while Civic Works conducted 18 meetings focusing on infrastructure projects, road maintenance, utilities, and public works.

The committee structure reveals how municipal councils systematically address their key responsibilities: approving annual budgets and financial plans, making zoning and land-use decisions that shape urban development, maintaining and expanding infrastructure like roads and water systems, and ensuring adequate community services from police and fire protection to libraries and recreation facilities. With only 2 full City Council meetings compared to 120 committee meetings, this structure allows for detailed examination of complex issues in specialized committees before final decisions are made by the full council, ensuring thorough debate on matters affecting residents' daily lives from property taxes to neighborhood development.
