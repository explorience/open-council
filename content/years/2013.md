London City Council's 2013 work demonstrates the extensive coordination required to govern a major municipality, with 122 committee meetings spread across eight specialized bodies. The Corporate Services Committee was the most active with 30 meetings, handling critical administrative functions like human resources, information technology, and financial management. The Planning and Environment Committee followed closely with 26 meetings, addressing zoning applications, development approvals, environmental assessments, and long-term urban planning initiatives that shape the city's growth and sustainability.

The remaining committees tackled equally vital municipal responsibilities: Community and Protective Services (22 meetings) managed public safety, social services, and community programs; Civic Works (18 meetings) oversaw infrastructure projects like roads, water systems, and public facilities; and Strategic Priorities and Policy (13 meetings) focused on long-term vision and major policy initiatives. The Budget Committee's 4 meetings, while fewer in number, were crucial for setting the city's financial direction and tax rates. With only 2 full City Council meetings, most detailed work occurred at the committee level, where councillors could thoroughly examine complex issues before bringing recommendations to the broader council for final approval.
