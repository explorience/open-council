London City Council's 2013 meeting schedule reveals the intensive, year-round nature of municipal governance, with 122 meetings held across eight different committees and council sessions. The Corporate Services Committee was the most active with 30 meetings, handling administrative matters, human resources, and financial oversight, while the Planning and Environment Committee held 26 meetings to address zoning applications, development proposals, and environmental policies. The Community and Protective Services Committee met 22 times to discuss public safety, social services, and community programs, while the Civic Works Committee's 18 meetings focused on infrastructure projects, roads, water systems, and public works.

The distribution of meetings reflects the core responsibilities of municipal government: managing day-to-day operations, planning for growth and development, maintaining infrastructure, and serving residents' needs. Specialized committees like the Budget Committee (4 meetings) concentrated on the annual budget process, while the Audit Committee (7 meetings) provided financial oversight. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee (13 meetings) addressed long-term planning and policy development. With only 2 full City Council meetings, most detailed work occurred at the committee level, where councillors could thoroughly examine issues before bringing recommendations to the full council for final decisions.
