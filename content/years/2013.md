London City Council's 2013 activities demonstrate the comprehensive scope of municipal governance, with 122 committee meetings addressing the diverse needs of the city. The Corporate Services Committee was the most active with 30 meetings, handling critical administrative functions like human resources, information technology, legal affairs, and financial management. The Planning and Environment Committee followed closely with 26 meetings, making crucial decisions about zoning applications, development approvals, environmental assessments, and long-term urban planning that shapes the city's growth and character.

The remaining committees reflect the breadth of municipal responsibilities, from the Community and Protective Services Committee's 22 meetings addressing public safety, emergency services, parks, recreation, and social programs, to the Civic Works Committee's 18 meetings focusing on infrastructure projects, road maintenance, water systems, and public works. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee met 13 times to establish long-term vision and policy direction, while specialized committees like Audit (7 meetings) and Budget (4 meetings) ensured financial oversight and fiscal planning. With only 2 full City Council meetings, most detailed work occurred at the committee level, where councillors could thoroughly examine issues before bringing recommendations to the broader council for final approval.
