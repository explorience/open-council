London City Council's 2013 work demonstrates the extensive and varied nature of municipal governance, with 122 meetings held across eight specialized committees throughout the year. The Corporate Services Committee was the most active with 30 meetings, handling crucial administrative functions like human resources, information technology, and financial management. The Planning and Environment Committee followed closely with 26 meetings, addressing zoning applications, development approvals, environmental assessments, and urban planning initiatives that shape the city's growth and sustainability.

The distribution of meetings reflects the breadth of municipal responsibilities, from the Community and Protective Services Committee's 22 meetings focused on social services, public health, fire protection, and bylaw enforcement, to the Civic Works Committee's 18 meetings dealing with infrastructure projects, roads, water systems, and public works. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee met 13 times to establish long-term vision and policy direction, while specialized bodies like the Audit Committee (7 meetings) and Budget Committee (4 meetings) ensured financial oversight and fiscal planning. The relatively few full City Council meetings (2) suggest that much of the detailed work occurred at the committee level, with council meetings reserved for final decisions and major policy matters.
