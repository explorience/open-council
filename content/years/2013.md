London City Council's 2013 work demonstrates the extensive deliberative process required to govern a major city, with 122 committee meetings held throughout the year. The Corporate Services Committee was the most active with 30 meetings, handling crucial administrative functions, human resources, and financial oversight. The Planning and Environment Committee followed closely with 26 meetings, addressing zoning applications, development approvals, environmental assessments, and long-term urban planning initiatives that shape the city's growth and sustainability.

The remaining committees each played vital roles in municipal governance: Community and Protective Services (22 meetings) dealt with public safety, emergency services, social programs, and community development; Civic Works (18 meetings) focused on infrastructure projects, public works, transportation, and utilities; while the Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee (13 meetings) handled high-level policy development and strategic planning. The specialized Audit Committee met 7 times to ensure financial accountability and proper oversight of city operations, while the Budget Committee's 4 meetings concentrated on the intensive process of developing and approving the annual municipal budget. Notably, only 2 formal City Council meetings were held, suggesting most detailed work occurred at the committee level before reaching full council for final approval.
