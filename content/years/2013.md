London City Council's 2013 work demonstrates the extensive scope of municipal governance, with 122 committee meetings addressing the full spectrum of city operations. The Corporate Services Committee led activity with 30 meetings, handling critical administrative functions like human resources, finance, and IT systems that keep the city running. The Planning and Environment Committee followed closely with 26 meetings, making crucial decisions on zoning applications, development approvals, environmental policies, and long-term city planning that shape London's growth and character.

The remaining committees tackled equally important areas of municipal responsibility. Community and Protective Services (22 meetings) addressed public safety, emergency services, parks and recreation, and social programs. Civic Works (18 meetings) focused on infrastructure decisions including road construction, water systems, waste management, and public transit. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee (13 meetings) worked on long-term planning and policy development, while specialized committees like Audit (7 meetings) and Budget (4 meetings) ensured financial oversight and annual budget approval. Remarkably, City Council itself met only twice, suggesting most detailed work occurred at the committee level before final ratification.
