London City Council maintained an active governance schedule in 2013 with 122 committee and council meetings throughout the year. The Corporate Services Committee was the busiest with 30 meetings, handling administrative matters, human resources, and financial oversight, followed closely by the Planning and Environment Committee with 26 meetings dedicated to zoning applications, development approvals, and environmental policy decisions. The Community and Protective Services Committee met 22 times to address public safety, social services, and community programs, while the Civic Works Committee's 18 meetings focused on infrastructure projects, road maintenance, and public works initiatives.

The meeting distribution reflects the typical priorities of municipal government, with significant time devoted to planning and development decisions that shape the city's growth, corporate governance that ensures efficient operations, and community services that directly impact residents' daily lives. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee's 13 meetings addressed long-term planning and policy development, while specialized committees like Audit (7 meetings) and Budget (4 meetings) provided focused oversight on financial accountability and annual budget deliberations. With only 2 formal City Council meetings, most decision-making occurred at the committee level before final ratification, demonstrating London's committee-based governance structure.
