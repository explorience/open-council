London City Council's 2013 work demonstrates the extensive and varied nature of municipal governance, with 122 meetings held across eight specialized committees throughout the year. The Corporate Services Committee was particularly active with 30 meetings, handling essential administrative functions like human resources, finance, and technology services. The Planning and Environment Committee, with 26 meetings, focused on critical urban development decisions including zoning changes, development applications, and environmental policies that shape the city's growth and sustainability.

The remaining committees addressed London's diverse municipal responsibilities through targeted expertise. The Community and Protective Services Committee (22 meetings) dealt with public safety, social services, and community programs, while Civic Works (18 meetings) managed infrastructure projects like roads, water systems, and public works. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee (13 meetings) provided long-term planning oversight, and specialized committees handled financial oversight (Audit Committee, 7 meetings) and budget deliberations (Budget Committee, 4 meetings). Notably, only 2 full City Council meetings were held, suggesting most detailed work occurred at the committee level before reaching council for final decisions on matters ranging from annual budgets and tax rates to development approvals and infrastructure investments.
