London City Council's 2013 work demonstrates the extensive deliberative process required to govern a major municipality, with 122 committee and council meetings held throughout the year. The Corporate Services Committee was the most active with 30 meetings, handling critical administrative functions like human resources, information technology, and financial management. The Planning and Environment Committee followed closely with 26 meetings, addressing zoning applications, development proposals, environmental assessments, and urban planning initiatives that shape the city's growth and sustainability.

The remaining committees tackled equally important municipal responsibilities: Community and Protective Services (22 meetings) oversaw public safety, social services, and community programs; Civic Works (18 meetings) managed infrastructure projects, roads, utilities, and public works; while Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee (13 meetings) focused on long-term planning and policy development. The Audit Committee's 7 meetings ensured financial oversight and accountability, while the Budget Committee's 4 intensive meetings culminated in approving the city's annual budget. Notably, only 2 full City Council meetings were held, suggesting most detailed work occurred at the committee level before final ratification.
