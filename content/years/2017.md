London City Council's 2017 work demonstrates the extensive and varied nature of municipal governance, with 104 committee meetings held across eight different committees throughout the year. The Corporate Services Committee was the most active with 25 meetings, handling administrative matters, human resources, and financial oversight, followed closely by the Planning and Environment Committee's 20 meetings focused on development applications, zoning changes, and environmental initiatives. The Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee met 19 times to address long-term planning and policy development, while the Civic Works Committee's 16 meetings dealt with infrastructure projects, roads, and public works.

The distribution of meetings reflects the core responsibilities of municipal government: managing city operations, reviewing development proposals and land use planning, maintaining and upgrading infrastructure, and providing community services and public safety through the Community and Protective Services Committee's 15 meetings. Interestingly, formal City Council and Budget Committee meetings were minimal at just one each, suggesting that much of the detailed work occurred at the committee level before items reached full council. The Audit Committee's seven meetings provided financial oversight and accountability, ensuring proper governance and transparency in the city's operations and spending decisions.
