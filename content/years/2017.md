London City Council's 2017 work demonstrates the comprehensive scope of municipal governance, with 104 committee meetings addressing the full spectrum of city operations. The Corporate Services Committee led activity with 25 meetings, focusing on financial management, human resources, and administrative policy. Planning and Environment Committee's 20 meetings handled crucial zoning decisions, development applications, and environmental initiatives, while the Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee's 19 sessions shaped long-term city direction and policy frameworks.

Infrastructure and community services formed another major pillar of council work, with the Civic Works Committee's 16 meetings addressing roads, utilities, and public works projects, and the Community and Protective Services Committee's 15 meetings covering public safety, social services, and community programs. The relatively few Audit Committee meetings (7) provided financial oversight, while the single Budget Committee meeting and full City Council meeting likely represented formal ratification sessions for major decisions developed through the committee system. This structure reflects typical municipal priorities: managing taxpayer resources, regulating land use and development, maintaining infrastructure, and delivering essential services to residents.
