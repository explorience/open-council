London City Council's 2017 work demonstrates the extensive committee structure required to govern a modern city, with 104 meetings held across eight specialized committees. The Corporate Services Committee was the most active with 25 meetings, handling critical administrative functions like human resources, information technology, and financial management. The Planning and Environment Committee followed closely with 20 meetings, reflecting the ongoing demands of urban development, zoning applications, and environmental assessments that are essential to managing London's growth and sustainability.

The distribution of meetings across committees reveals the breadth of municipal responsibilities. Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee's 19 meetings focused on long-term planning and policy development, while Civic Works Committee's 16 meetings addressed infrastructure needs like roads, water systems, and public works projects. The Community and Protective Services Committee held 15 meetings to oversee public safety, social services, and community programs. Notably, the Budget Committee met only once, likely for the annual budget approval process, while the Audit Committee's 7 meetings ensured financial oversight and accountability throughout the year.
