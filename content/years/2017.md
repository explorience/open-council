London City Council's 2017 work demonstrates the extensive scope of municipal governance, with 104 committee meetings addressing the diverse needs of the city. The Corporate Services Committee was the most active with 25 meetings, handling crucial administrative matters such as human resources, information technology, legal affairs, and financial management. The Planning and Environment Committee followed closely with 20 meetings, reviewing development applications, zoning changes, environmental assessments, and urban planning initiatives that shape the city's growth and sustainability.

The remaining committees each played vital roles in municipal decision-making: Strategic Priorities and Policy Committee (19 meetings) focused on long-term vision and policy development, while Civic Works Committee (16 meetings) addressed infrastructure projects including roads, water systems, and public works. The Community and Protective Services Committee (15 meetings) oversaw social services, public health, emergency services, and community programs. The Audit Committee's 7 meetings ensured financial accountability and transparency. Notably, both the Budget Committee and City Council itself held only one formal meeting each, suggesting that budget deliberations and final council decisions were consolidated into focused sessions, while most of the detailed work occurred at the committee level.
